What Does a Professional Music Promotion Campaign Include?

"What does a music promotion campaign actually include?" is a question artists and managers should be able to answer before they sign anything. A good campaign is a plan with a start date, a lead single, a clear set of lanes and honest reporting. It is not a promise of streams.

The short answer

A professional campaign usually covers the story, the timing, and coordinated work across press, playlists, radio or podcasts, and often short-form content or video. MusicPromoToday plans and runs this kind of work for artists and labels, including music PR, Spotify promotion, radio and podcasts and YouTube music promotion. The right mix depends on the release, the genre and the territory, not on a packaged guarantee.

The pieces you should expect to see

1. A brief, not just a start date

What is the release, who is it for, and what would count as a good result? If this is not written down, the campaign has no centre.

2. Assets that are actually ready

Finished audio, artwork, a short bio, photos, a one-click listen link and, where relevant, a clean edit or video. Promotion cannot invent these later without losing time.

3. Press that is pitched, not promised

Writers and editors are asked, not booked as a guaranteed review. You should know the kinds of outlets being approached and why they fit.

4. Playlist and curator work with taste in mind

Editorial pitching and independent curators are different jobs from buying a slot. A serious company will say which of those it is doing.

5. Radio and podcasts where they make sense

Specialty, college, community or format radio, and podcasts that actually play or discuss new music, should be chosen for the genre, not added as a default line.

6. A content plan, if the audience lives on short video

A campaign that ignores the way fans already find songs is incomplete. The content should sound like the artist, not like a template.

7. Reporting you can read

You should be told what was sent, who replied, what ran, and what happens next. Vanity charts without context are not a report.

Questions worth asking before you start

  • Which lanes are in this campaign, and which are not?
  • Who will see the music, in plain terms?
  • How do you handle a quiet first week?
  • What will I receive each week or at the end?
  • What is not included in the fee?

The guide to choosing a music promotion company sets these questions out in more detail.

What a professional campaign will not include

  • A guarantee of chart positions, streams or reviews.
  • Requests for your streaming-service passwords.
  • A single mass email dressed up as a full plan.

Bottom line

A real promotion campaign is coordinated work around a release, with clear lanes and clear reporting. MusicPromoToday builds that plan with artists and labels and runs it across press, playlists, radio, video and the other lanes a release actually needs.
